# Canberra Refugee Support — verified fact sheet

Gathered 10 August 2026 from the organisation's own site: `/`, `/about/`,
`/volunteer/`, and the referrals page. Third-party directories were checked
but NOT used as sources — see "Conflicts" below.

Source of record: https://canberrarefugee.org.au

## Identity

- Canberra Refugee Support Inc. (CRS)
- Not-for-profit, 100% volunteer. No office, no employees.
- Incorporated in the ACT, publicly accountable.
- Member of the Refugee Council of Australia and ACT Council of Social Services.
- Registered charity with the ACNC; ATO-recognised, donations tax deductible.
- No government program funding. Membership fees cover overheads, so donations
  go directly to the people supported.

## What they do

Core model: "good neighbour" support — CRS matches small groups of volunteers
to asylum seekers and refugees registered with the organisation, providing
longer-term social support shaped by the family's needs and the volunteers'
availability.

- Practical support and referral to services: accommodation, furniture, local
  services, schooling, English language training, health care, jobs, networks.
- Helping people access services they are already entitled to.
- Facilitating contact with policy makers on equity and access.
- Scholarships and mentoring programs.
- Limited direct financial support, ONLY for people registered with CRS.

Scale, in their own framing: thousands of volunteer hours a year, and tens of
thousands of dollars annually toward rent and essential living expenses, mostly
donated by Canberrans.

## Who they prioritise

No strict eligibility criteria. Stated priorities:

1. Asylum seekers on temporary visas ineligible for much government assistance.
2. Recently arrived refugees facing significant settlement challenges.

High-need cases outside these priorities are not ruled out where capacity exists.

## Referrals — how someone gets support

IMPORTANT: the published process is written for a THIRD PARTY referring someone,
not for self-referral.

1. Email info@canberrarefugee.org.au with details of the person being referred.
2. A CRS volunteer support coordinator makes contact.
3. Coordinators meet the referred people to explain what CRS offers, learn their
   needs, and seek agreement to proceed.
4. The referral goes to the CRS committee for confirmation.
5. If confirmed, CRS seeks volunteers from its membership, briefs them, and
   introduces them.

Helpful details in a referral (not all required): name, age, family composition,
suburb, country of origin, visa type, contact details, language spoken at home,
English level, time in Australia or Canberra, type of assistance needed.

## Volunteering

Volunteers are drawn from CRS members. Roles:

- Good neighbour support team for a particular family or individual.
- One-off requests: house moves, job search, household items, transport to
  appointments, garden working bees, sport, tutoring.
- Fundraising.
- The scholarship program.
- Administrative tasks and projects.
- The CRS committee (governance).

No prior experience or skills required. Volunteers are supported by the
committee and by volunteer support coordinators.

Steps to volunteer:

1. Become a CRS member.
2. Attend an orientation session (before or after joining).
3. Apply for a Working with Vulnerable People (WWVP) card for an active support
   role. Free when for volunteer work only.
4. Watch for volunteer requests emailed to members, or email CRS directly.

WWVP applications should name: Membership Secretary, CRS, PO Box 3442,
Weston Creek ACT 2611.

Current vacancy (as at the date above): Fundraising Coordinator, about one hour
a week, with support and documentation from experienced committee members.

## Education Awards

Scholarships presented during Refugee Week each year to asylum seeker and
refugee children and adults, nominated by participating organisations. Run by a
small volunteer team.
